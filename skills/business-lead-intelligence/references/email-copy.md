# Email copy review

The generated draft is a starting point, not finished copy. Load the
`cold-email` skill and rewrite the draft until it sounds like one person writing
to another. Keep every factual claim traceable to the report.

## Cold Outreach Writing Standard

When drafting or reviewing cold emails for my web design and redesign outreach,
prioritize natural, specific, persuasive writing.

### Understand the prospect first

- Use the actual audit findings and business context.
- Identify one meaningful problem, not a list of defects.
- Explain why it matters to this particular business.
- Never invent research, customer behaviour, results, or urgency.

### Sound like a person

- Use simple, conversational English and contractions.
- Avoid corporate language, generic compliments, and sales jargon.
- Do not make every email follow the same sentence structure.
- Avoid robotic openings such as "I noticed something" and generic claims like
  "unlock your business potential."
- Avoid explaining obvious things to the recipient.
- Keep the tone calm, direct, and low-pressure.

### Make the email persuasive

- Don't merely report a technical issue. Connect it to a plausible customer
  experience or business consideration.
- Distinguish measured facts from assumptions.
- Show sound judgment about which issue matters most.
- If a custom landing page concept exists, explain what the prospect can
  actually see or evaluate in it.
- Sell the relevance of the idea, not the fact that it is free.
- Never claim the concept has improved the live website.

### Avoid template thinking

- Do not force every email into the same problem-solution-CTA formula.
- Vary the angle according to the prospect's actual situation.
- Do not repeat the same observation in follow-ups.
- Write the subject after deciding the email's main point.

### Before returning a draft

1. Remove any sentence that could be sent unchanged to 100 unrelated
   businesses.
2. Remove claims that are not supported by the available data.
3. Check whether the recipient has a real reason to reply.
4. Ask whether the email sounds natural when read aloud.
5. Prefer a clear, specific message over clever sales copy.
6. If the idea is weak, say so instead of disguising it with better wording.
7. Return the final email and briefly explain the reasoning.

The standard above is the governing checklist. The rest of this file adds
concrete rules and examples for applying it.

## First-touch shape

Use a short, natural email:

1. **One specific observation.** A load time, a broken link, a missing address,
   a mixed-content warning, a review count. Use the real number.
2. **Why it matters to the patient.** One sentence. Do not add statistics you
   cannot verify.
3. **One offer.** A free landing page concept, a short fix list, or a review
   section. One ask only.
4. **Signature.** Real name and postal address.
5. **Natural opt-out.** A human sentence, not a legal-looking footer.

Aim for 50–90 words. Cut anything that does not move the reader toward a reply.

## Free landing page offer

A free landing page concept is a strong offer because the reader can see the
work. It is only honest if the concept exists.

- **If the concept exists:** "I built a free landing page concept for [business].
  Want me to send the link?"
- **If it does not exist yet:** "I can build a free landing page concept for
  [business]. Want me to?" Do not say you built it.
- **Do not put the link in the first email.** Ask permission first. It protects
  deliverability and filters for real interest.
- **Keep a URL per business.** When someone replies, send the matching link.
- **The concept must use verified business details.** Name, phone, address,
  rating, review count, and services may come from the report or the business's
  public site. Do not invent reviews, awards, or patient numbers.
- **Mark it as a concept.** Put a small footer on the page: "Free landing page
  concept prepared for [business] by [name]. This is a concept, not the live
  site."

## Natural opt-out lines

Do not use the same robotic line in every email. Rotate natural variants:

- "If this isn't useful, just say so and I'll stop."
- "No worries if it's not for you, just let me know."
- "If you'd rather not hear from me, say so and I'll leave it there."
- "If this isn't a fit, just tell me and I'll stop."
- "Not for you? Just say so and I'll leave it."

Keep an opt-out in every email. Keep the postal address in the signature.

## Voice

Write like a peer who noticed something useful:

- Use contractions: "doesn't", "that's", "I've".
- Vary the opening. Do not start every email with "I ran a quick audit."
- Lead with their world, not your service.
- Use one concrete detail, not a list of features.
- Avoid "I hope this email finds you well", "quick question", "synergy",
  "leverage", "best-in-class", and "circle back".
- Avoid contrast reveals ("It's not X, it's Y"), em dashes, and three-item lists
  that sound generated.
- Read it aloud. If it sounds like a template, rewrite it.

## Subject lines

- 2–4 words, lowercase.
- Internal-looking, not a pitch: `cantt load time`, `smileon address`,
  `dentocorrect reviews`.
- No fake `Re:` or `Fwd:`, no emojis, no prospect first name.

## Formatting

- Plain text by default.
- No HTML, bold, images, tracking pixels, or multiple links in the first touch.
- One link only after the prospect asks for it.
- Keep the signature short: name, postal address, opt-out.

## Before sending

- Does the first line name something real about this business?
- Would you reply to it?
- Is the offer easy to understand in one read?
- Is there exactly one ask?
- Is the postal address present?
- Is the opt-out natural and present?
- Is every claim supported by the report or the business's public site?
- If the email claims a mockup exists, does the mockup actually exist and does
  the URL work?
- Does the email avoid the words and patterns listed above?

## Example rewrite

**Robotic:**

> Hi team,
>
> You have 4.9 stars from 777 Google reviews. The homepage takes about 6.2
> seconds to respond.
>
> On a phone, a 6.2-second wait is a long time before a patient sees your
> services.
>
> I can send the two changes that would speed it up most. Want them?
>
> Not relevant? Reply "no" and I won't follow up.

**Human:**

> Hi team,
>
> The Cantt Dental Care homepage takes about 6 seconds to load on a phone.
> That's long enough for someone to leave before they see your services, and one
> of the links on the page is broken.
>
> I built a free landing page concept for Cantt with a faster first screen and a
> contact route that works. Want me to send the link?
>
> Pius Shedrach
> Port Harcourt 500001, Rivers State, Nigeria
>
> If this isn't useful, just say so and I'll stop.

The second version is shorter, names a real problem, offers something visible,
and asks for permission instead of pushing a fix list.
